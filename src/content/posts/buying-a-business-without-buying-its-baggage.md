Buying an existing business can be a great way to skip the start-up stage. You may be acquiring customers, equipment, employees, contracts, and a business that is already up and running. The important question is what else may come with it. {.summary}

One of the first decisions is whether you are buying the assets of the business or the shares of the corporation. 

With an asset purchase, you choose which assets you are buying, such as equipment, inventory, intellectual property, contracts, or the business name. This can give you more control over what you take on, but it does not eliminate every risk. Contracts may need to be assigned, landlords or lenders may need to consent, employees require careful consideration, and equipment or other property may be subject to registered liens. 

With a share purchase, you buy the corporation itself. The company continues to own its assets and remain responsible for its existing obligations. That means its debts, contracts, employee obligations, tax history, litigation, and other liabilities generally stay with the corporation after the sale. 

This is why due diligence matters. Before closing, you should understand what the business owns, what it owes, what agreements it is bound by, and whether any contracts contain change-of-control provisions. 

Tax can also affect which structure makes sense, so your accountant should be involved before the deal is finalized. 

There is no single right way to buy a business. The goal is to understand exactly what is included in the deal and what may be coming along with it.
