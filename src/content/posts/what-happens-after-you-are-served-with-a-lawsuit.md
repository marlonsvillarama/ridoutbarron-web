<p class="summary">
Being served with a lawsuit can be unsettling, particularly if you were not expecting it. The most important thing to know is that being sued does not mean the other side has already proven its case. It means a legal process has started, and you now have an opportunity to respond.
</p>

The first step is to review the documents carefully and take note of when and how you were served. In Alberta, the deadline to respond depends on the court and where service took place. For example, a defendant served in Alberta with a Statement of Claim in the Court of King’s Bench generally has 20 days to file and serve a Statement of Defence. Different timelines apply when service occurs outside Alberta. 

If you have been served with a Civil Claim in the Alberta Court of Justice, you generally have 20 days to file a Dispute Note if you were served in Alberta, or 30 days if you were served outside the province. 

For that reason, it is important to speak with a lawyer promptly. Bring the documents you were served with, along with any contracts, correspondence, invoices, photographs, or other records that relate to the dispute. Your lawyer can review the claim, identify the issues being raised, and determine what response is appropriate. 

That response may involve filing a defence, raising a counterclaim, bringing another party into the action, or exploring an early resolution. The right approach will depend on the allegations, the available evidence, and what you are trying to achieve.

> Ignoring the claim is rarely a good option.

If you do not respond within the applicable timeframe, the plaintiff may be able to seek judgment without your participation. In the Alberta Court of Justice, that can lead to a default judgment and enforcement steps such as garnishment or seizure of assets. 

You do not need to have your entire defence figured out before speaking with a lawyer. You simply need to act early enough to preserve your options.
