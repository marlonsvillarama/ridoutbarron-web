Meeting with a lawyer for the first time can feel daunting, particularly when you are already dealing with a dispute. Our goal is to make the process straightforward and to ensure that your consultation is focused on the issues that matter most. {.summary}

When you contact Ridout Barron to request a consultation, a member of our team will gather some initial information about your matter, help coordinate the next steps, and arrange a meeting with one of our lawyers by Microsoft Teams. 

Before the consultation, we will ask you to provide documents that help explain the dispute. These may include contracts, correspondence, court materials, or other records that provide useful background. 

Providing these materials in advance is an important part of the process. It gives the lawyer an opportunity to review the history of the matter before meeting with you, identify the issues that may require closer attention, and make better use of your consultation time. The meeting can then focus on your questions, your legal position, and the options available to you rather than simply reconstructing the background from the beginning. 

You do not need to determine which documents are legally significant before sending them to us. If a document helps explain what happened or provides context for the dispute, it may be useful. We can help identify what requires further attention once the materials have been reviewed. 

During the consultation, the lawyer will usually ask questions to clarify the facts and better understand what you are hoping to achieve. The discussion may include the strengths and weaknesses of your position, any immediate concerns or deadlines, the evidence available, and the practical considerations that may affect how the matter should proceed. 

The consultation is also an opportunity to discuss what moving forward may involve, including the likely scope of the work, potential costs, and what you can reasonably expect from the process. 

You are not expected to leave the meeting with every decision made. The purpose of the consultation is to give you a clearer understanding of where you stand and enough information to decide what makes sense from there.
