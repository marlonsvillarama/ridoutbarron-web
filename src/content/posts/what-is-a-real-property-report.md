A Real Property Report, or RPR, is a legal document prepared by an Alberta Land Surveyor. It shows the boundaries of a property and the location of visible improvements, such as the house, garage, deck, fence, shed, or retaining wall. 

An RPR can reveal issues that may not be obvious from looking at the property. For example, it may show that a structure encroaches onto a neighbouring property or onto land belonging to the City, or that an improvement does not match the current layout of the property. 

When a home is being sold, the RPR is often used to confirm that the improvements shown on the property comply with municipal requirements. In Calgary, sellers may also need a Certificate of Compliance from the City confirming whether those improvements comply with the applicable Land Use Bylaw. 

An RPR does not technically expire, but it can become outdated. If a deck, garage, fence, addition, or other improvement has been added or changed since the RPR was prepared, it may need to be updated before it can be relied on. 

> If you are selling a property and do not have a current, compliant RPR, that issue should be addressed as early as possible.

Depending on the terms of the purchase contract, a holdback may be placed on your sale proceeds until an acceptable RPR and certificate of compliance can be provided. 

Title insurance may sometimes be used instead of a current RPR, depending on the purchase contract, lender requirements, and circumstances of the transaction. Your lawyer can explain whether that is an option in your particular case. 

An RPR may look like a simple drawing of your lot, but it can have a very real impact on whether your sale is ready to complete. 

So if you are thinking about selling, it is worth finding that RPR before you start packing boxes.
