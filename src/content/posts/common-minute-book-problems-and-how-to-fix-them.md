<p class="summary">
A corporate minute book is meant to be the official record of your company. It should show how the corporation was set up, who the directors and shareholders are, what shares have been issued, and what important decisions have been made over time.
</p>

In real life, minute books do not always stay perfectly up to date. Annual resolutions may go unsigned, a director may change without the Corporate Registry filing being updated, or share certificates may be missing. Sometimes the minute book simply no longer matches what everyone understands to be true about the company. That can become a problem when someone suddenly needs to rely on those records. 

The good news is that many of these problems can be fixed. 

The usual starting point is to compare the minute book with the corporation’s current filings and whatever supporting records are available. From there, missing resolutions can often be prepared, outdated filings can be corrected, and the records can be brought back into line. 

Some issues are more complicated, especially if there is uncertainty about ownership, past share issuances, or decisions that were never properly documented. In those cases, more information may be needed before anything is changed. 

If your minute book has been sitting untouched for a while, it is probably worth giving it a check before you actually need it for something important.
