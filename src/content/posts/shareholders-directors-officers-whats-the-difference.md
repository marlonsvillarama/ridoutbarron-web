When a business is incorporated, the same person may wear several different hats. You can be a shareholder, a director, and an officer of the same corporation, but those roles are not interchangeable. {.summary}

A shareholder is an owner of the corporation. Shareholders hold shares and may have voting, dividend, or other rights depending on the class of shares they own. They generally make certain major decisions, such as electing directors or approving matters that require shareholder approval. 

A director helps govern the corporation. Alberta corporations must have at least one director, and directors are responsible for overseeing the corporation’s affairs and making higher-level decisions about how it is run. Directors also have legal duties to the corporation and must exercise their powers in accordance with Alberta’s Business Corporations Act. 

An officer is usually involved in the day-to-day management of the business. Titles may include President, Secretary, Treasurer, Chief Executive Officer, or another position created by the corporation. Officers are generally appointed by the directors, and their responsibilities depend on the role they have been given. 

In a small business, one person may hold all three roles. For example, you may own all of the shares, act as the sole director, and serve as President. The distinction still matters because you may be acting in a different legal capacity depending on the decision being made. 

This becomes especially important when preparing corporate records. Shareholder resolutions, director resolutions, officer appointments, share issuances, and other corporate documents need to reflect who is actually authorized to make each decision. 

If your corporation has gone through changes in ownership, directors, or officers, your records need to tell the same story.
