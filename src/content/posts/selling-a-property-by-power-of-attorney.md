Sometimes a property owner cannot handle a sale personally and another person needs to act on their behalf under a Power of Attorney. 

In Alberta, a Power of Attorney allows a person, or several persons, called the attorney or attorneys, to make financial decisions for another person, called the donor. An Enduring Power of Attorney can continue to operate if the donor later loses capacity, depending on how the document is written and when it takes effect. 

Before a property can be sold, the Power of Attorney needs to be reviewed carefully. As a standard practice at Ridout Barron, we require the original Power of Attorney to be provided to us so that our estates lawyers can review it and confirm that it gives the attorney sufficient authority to complete the transaction. 

We will notarize the Power of Attorney in-house and submit the notarial copy to Alberta Land Titles as part of the registration process. The original Power of Attorney will be returned to you. 

> If the Power of Attorney only becomes effective after a particular event, such as the donor losing capacity, additional documentation may also be required before the attorney can act. 

The attorney must act within the authority given by the document and for the benefit of the donor. The transfer documents also need to be signed in the proper capacity so that Land Titles can register the sale correctly. 

It is also important to remember that a Power of Attorney only operates while the donor is alive. If the registered owner dies before the transaction is completed, the attorney can no longer act under that authority and the property must instead be dealt with through the estate. 

If you are planning to sell a property under a Power of Attorney, send us the original document as early as possible so that we can review it before the transaction gets too far along. 

A little paperwork at the beginning can save a much bigger scramble at the end.
