When someone passes away, the legal and administrative steps that follow can feel like a lot to take on at once. {.summary}

In Alberta, a grant is a court document that confirms who has authority to deal with an estate. The type of grant usually depends on whether the deceased left a valid Will and who will be responsible for administering the estate. 

If there is a valid Will and the person named as executor is able and willing to act, the application is generally for a Grant of Probate. Probate confirms the executor’s authority to carry out the instructions in the Will and deal with estate assets. 

If there is no Will, the application is generally for a Grant of Administration. In that situation, Alberta law determines who may apply to administer the estate, usually based on the deceased’s family relationships. 

Not every estate requires a grant. Whether one is needed depends on what the deceased owned, how those assets were held, and what the banks, investment companies, Land Titles, or other institutions involved require. 

> If a grant is necessary, the application usually includes information about the deceased, the estate assets, the beneficiaries, and the person asking to act for the estate.
