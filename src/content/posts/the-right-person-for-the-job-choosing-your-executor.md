Choosing an executor is one of the most important decisions you will make when preparing your Will. {.summary}

Your executor is the person responsible for carrying out your wishes after you die. That can include locating and securing assets, dealing with banks and investments, paying debts and taxes, applying for probate if necessary, selling property, communicating with beneficiaries, and eventually distributing the estate. 

A good executor should be organized, trustworthy, comfortable dealing with paperwork, and able to make decisions when needed. It also helps if they are someone who can communicate calmly with family members and beneficiaries, particularly if there is any possibility of disagreement. 

You should also consider where the person lives and whether they realistically have the time to take on the role. An executor does not need to live in Alberta, but having someone who is readily available can make certain parts of the administration easier. 

It is usually a good idea to name an alternate executor as well. If your first choice predeceases you, becomes unable to act, or simply does not want the responsibility when the time comes, an alternate can step in. 

> You can also appoint more than one executor, but that does not always make things simpler.

Co-executors may need to make decisions together and sign documents jointly, so it is worth considering whether the people you choose are likely to work well together. 

If you do not have a family member or friend who is a good fit, you may also consider appointing a professional executor. This can include a trust company or another professional estate administration service that agrees in advance to act as executor for a fee. 

You will want to confirm that the company is willing to accept the appointment, understand how its fees are calculated, and find out whether there are any minimum estate values or other requirements. 

Before naming an individual executor, speak with them as well. They should know that you are considering them for the role and have a general idea of what would be expected. 

The right executor does not have to know everything about estates. They simply need to be someone, or an institution, you trust to take the job seriously and get the right help when needed.
