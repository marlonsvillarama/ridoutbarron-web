<p class="summary">
Litigation rarely moves in a straight line. There may be periods of significant activity followed by stretches when documents are being exchanged, responses are outstanding, settlement discussions are underway, or the next procedural step is still some time away.
</p>

That does not necessarily mean your file has stalled. Civil litigation can take time. In Alberta, the Court of King’s Bench has established a target of moving non-family civil actions to trial within 36 months of the first Statement of Defence, although many matters resolve before reaching trial and more complex cases may take longer. 

> Your lawyer is responsible for managing the legal process, monitoring deadlines, and determining what procedural steps should be taken.

There are, however, a few things you can do to stay engaged and help keep your matter moving. We like to think of them as CHECK. 

## C — Communicate with your lawyer. 
Keep your lawyer informed when something relevant changes, particularly if there are new communications, documents, developments, or circumstances that could affect the dispute. 

## H — Have your documents organized. 
Providing requested records promptly and keeping related correspondence and documents together can make it easier to respond when the file requires action. 

## E — Expect periods of waiting. 
Not every quiet period is a delay. The other side may have time to respond, court dates may need to be scheduled, or the parties may be working through required steps before the matter can advance. 

## C — Check in when you are unsure. 
If you have not heard anything for some time and do not know what is happening, ask. A brief update can often explain where the matter stands and what the next step will be. 

## K — Keep the objective in mind. 
Litigation can become consuming. Returning to the reason you started the process can help when decisions about settlement, cost, or strategy arise along the way. 

A well-managed litigation file will not necessarily move quickly, but it should move deliberately. You should understand where your matter stands, what the next significant step is, and what your lawyer needs from you when the time comes.
