In Alberta, a handwritten Will can be valid, but that does not always mean it is a good estate plan. {.summary}

A handwritten Will is known as a holograph Will. Under Alberta’s Wills and Succession Act, it must be written entirely in the testator’s own handwriting and signed by the testator. Unlike a formal Will, a holograph Will does not require witnesses. 

Although holograph Wills are legally recognized, we generally do not recommend preparing your own Will. The problem is not usually whether the document exists. The problem is whether it clearly and completely deals with your estate. 

A Will should appoint the person who will administer your estate, identify who should receive your property, account for your family circumstances, and address what should happen if a beneficiary dies before you or another part of your plan cannot be carried out as expected. 

Handwritten Wills can create uncertainty when important provisions are missing, the wording is unclear, or there is doubt about what the writer intended. Those issues are often discovered only after the person has passed away, when there is no opportunity to explain or correct the document. 

There can also be additional steps when a holograph Will is submitted to the Court. In some cases, an Affidavit of Handwriting may be required to confirm that the handwriting and signature belong to the deceased. 

If you find a handwritten Will after someone has passed away, keep the original document safe and do not write on it, attach anything to it, or alter it. Bring it to a lawyer so that it can be reviewed properly. 

If you are thinking about writing your own Will, our recommendation is to have one professionally prepared. A properly drafted Will can reduce uncertainty, make your wishes easier to carry out, and make things easier for your family later.
