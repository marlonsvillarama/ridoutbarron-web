<p class="summary">
Legal bills come with their own vocabulary, and two words you will see often are fees and disbursements.
</p>

Legal fees are what you pay for the professional work done on your matter. Depending on the type of file, those fees may be based on the time spent, a fixed fee, or another arrangement discussed with you at the beginning of the retainer. 

Disbursements are costs paid or incurred in connection with your file. These can include court filing fees, Land Titles charges, corporate registry fees, searches, courier costs, process servers, or other third-party expenses required to move the matter forward. 

You may also be asked to provide a retainer before work begins. A retainer is generally an advance toward future legal fees and expenses. Those funds are held in trust and applied to your account as work is completed and billed. 

We never want a legal bill to feel like something you need a law degree to understand. If you have a question about a charge, a disbursement, or how your retainer has been applied, ask us. We would much rather explain the account than have you staring at it wondering what something means.
