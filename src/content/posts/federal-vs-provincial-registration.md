<p class="summary">
If you are starting a business in Alberta, one of the first decisions you may run into is whether to incorporate provincially in Alberta or federally.
</p>

Both options create a corporation that is legally separate from you as an individual. The real difference is where the corporation is created, which rules it follows, and what registrations you may need afterward. 

If your business is going to operate mainly in Alberta, an Alberta corporation is often the simpler route. It is incorporated under Alberta’s Business Corporations Act and registered through Alberta Corporate Registry. From there, you will need to keep up with the usual corporate housekeeping, including annual returns and updates when certain corporate information changes. 

A federal corporation is incorporated under the Canada Business Corporations Act. This can make sense if you expect to do business in more than one province or want the broader name protection that comes with federal incorporation. 

> The catch is that federal incorporation does not replace provincial registration. 

If your federal corporation carries on business in Alberta, it will generally still need to register here as an extra-provincial corporation. In practical terms, that can mean dealing with both Corporations Canada and Alberta Corporate Registry. 

So, which one should you choose? 

If your business is staying fairly local, provincial incorporation may be perfectly suitable. If you are planning to expand across Canada, federal incorporation may be worth considering. The right answer really depends on where you expect the business to operate and how you want to structure it from the start.
