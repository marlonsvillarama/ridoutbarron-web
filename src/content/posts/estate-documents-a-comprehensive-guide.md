Estate planning is not only about what happens after you die. It is also about making sure the right people can step in if you are ever unable to make decisions for yourself. {.summary}

In Alberta, there are three core documents that work together:
1. a Will
2. a Power of Attorney, and 
3. a Personal Directive.
   
Each one serves a different purpose, and having all three in place can make things much easier for the people you trust if something unexpected happens. The Government of Alberta recommends that every adult have all three. 

A **Will* deals with your estate after your death. It allows you to decide how your property should be distributed, name the person who will administer your estate, and name a guardian for minor children. If you die without a Will, Alberta’s Wills and Succession Act determines how your estate is distributed instead. 

A good Will should reflect your current family circumstances, assets, and wishes. It is also worth reviewing after major life changes such as marriage, separation, the birth of a child, the death of a beneficiary or executor, or a significant change in your financial situation. 

A **Power of Attorney** deals with financial and legal decisions while you are still alive. It allows you to appoint someone you trust to act for you, either immediately or if you later lose capacity, depending on how the document is written. That authority can include managing bank accounts, paying bills, dealing with investments, or handling real estate. 

Without a Power of Attorney, a family member or friend may have to apply to Court to become your trustee if you lose the ability to manage your own financial affairs. That process can take time and involve additional expense. 

A **Personal Directive** deals with personal decisions rather than financial ones. It allows you to name an agent to make decisions for you if you lose capacity. Those decisions can include medical treatment, where you live, who provides your care, and other personal matters. 

A Personal Directive only comes into effect if you are found to lack capacity. It can also include written instructions about your wishes, which can provide useful guidance to your agent and healthcare providers. 

When choosing the people named in these documents, think beyond who is closest to you. Consider who is organized, trustworthy, comfortable making difficult decisions, and willing to take on the responsibility. It is also a good idea to speak with them before naming them so they understand what you are asking of them. 

Once your documents are signed, keep the originals somewhere secure but accessible. Your executor, attorney, and agent should know that the documents exist and where they can be found. Copies of your Personal Directive can also be provided to your agent, doctor, or other healthcare providers.
